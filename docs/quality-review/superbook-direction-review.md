The visual atmosphere comes from layered ochre rock, open sky, cast shadows, dust, textured clothing and metal, and soldiers staged at readable distances. Faces matter at least as much as background detail. The clip's modern witnesses, comic dialogue and extended close combat are Superbook's adaptation choices. The visual brief for our game is original illustrated 3D with the same clarity of intent, weight, cause and reaction. Fine soundtrack and mix details were outside this visual pass. ## What belongs in our episode

**1 Samuel 17** supplies the episode. The surrounding chapters explain David's place in Jesse's household, his anointing and prior service to Saul, and the relationship with Jonathan and Saul's jealousy after the victory. Use that context to inform the characters. The playable arc can begin with Jesse's errand and end with the Philistines' retreat and a short closing reaction. Anointing, court service and the later royal story can sit outside this episode. [1 Samuel 16–18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2016-18&version=NRSVUE)

| Story beat | Treatment for the first complete episode |
| --- | --- |
| Bethlehem and the errand, 17:12–22 | Keep a compact, lived-in opening. David hears Jesse's request once, takes the provisions and leaves. A short transition covers the journey and gets the player to camp. Mattan and the yard activity are editable adaptation material. |
| Challenge and decision, 17:1–11, 16, 23–37 | Reveal the opposing ridges and hear Goliath's challenge at camp. A short line can establish that he has been challenging Israel for forty days. Use one brief reaction from Eliab and one exchange with Saul to establish fear, David's resolve and his trust in God. |
| Armour and stones, 17:38–40 | Show David putting aside unfamiliar armour. Let the player gather the five stones in one deliberate interaction at the wadi and see the sling and pouch before entering the field. |
| Encounter, 17:41–50 | Include the taunt, David's answer about the Lord, his advance, the player's assisted sling release and the forehead strike. Any dodge or weapon pass is optional dramatic staging for the prototype. |
| Death and retreat, 17:51–58 | Make Goliath's death and the Philistines' flight clear. Propose a cutaway and brief closing line for the sword episode in verse 51. Keep the aftermath non-graphic and use Saul or Eliab for the last reaction if it helps close the arc. |

The approved target stays **10–15 minutes**, with the camp, preparation and encounter taking the larger share. Keep the fixed outcome, assisted aiming and in-session checkpoints. Use original dialogue and approve the exact wording and treatment of verse 51 before recording or final art. The source's height readings differ across textual traditions, so let the staging establish Goliath's scale without a numerical claim in the game. ## Proposed encounter design

**Space and cast.** Build one compact Elah arena with two ridgelines, a dry wadi, one crossing and a clear line of approach. Use a few posed or animated witness silhouettes for the armies. Start with David, Goliath and one readable witness. Give David a distinctive shepherd silhouette and sling. Give Goliath an original armoured silhouette, purposeful weapon poses and a rig that shows weight in his hips and shoulders. Prove one lighting setup from the actual gameplay and close-up cameras before producing many assets.

**Player control and camera.** Establish geography in a short wide, then reveal Goliath in a low-angle shot and give David a brief reaction. Return to a stable third-person view for the approach and assisted aim. One readable threat is enough for the prototype. A deliberate input releases the stone, with an equivalent touch action and clear ready feedback. Resume authored camera work after release for flight, impact, fall and a reaction. In portrait, reframe the subject and weapon path while keeping subtitles and the action target clear. Keep the existing ability to pause or skip dialogue with a safe handoff. Skipping a shot cannot count as the player's sling action.

**Animation and meaning.** Author the run, stop, windup, release and follow-through as connected motion. For Goliath, prioritize a threatening weight shift, a readable attack, shock, stagger and fall. Add a small tested set of eye, brow and jaw controls for the close shots. Use key animation events for sling release and contact so the stone, sound and reaction agree. Test first with footsteps, weapon movement, sling snap and the ground impact. Preserve the brief declaration of faith and a closing reaction that makes the victory's meaning clear.

## Earlier stack assumption

Keep **Godot 4.7.2, GDScript, Blender 4.5.15 LTS and GLB** for the browser proof. The project has working skeletal animation, a collision-aware camera, responsive controls and a single-threaded Godot Web export. Use the existing animation graph for controllable movement and authored clips for action cues. In the duel, let one small encounter controller validate player input and resolve the outcome once. Animation and camera take their cues from that state. Reuse that boundary when integrating C01 and C02. [Pinned tools](../implementation/a03-toolchain-setup-2026-10-29.md) · [Godot AnimationTree](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/animation/animation_tree.html)

The Web target is **Compatibility / WebGL 2 and WebAssembly**, served through the existing HTML shell. The old village's 15-minute laptop run averaged about 32 FPS. The separate vertex-lighting experiment brought median cold start from 43.5 to 9.4 seconds on the same laptop and changed the look of character highlights. The current village manifest records about 10.26 MB of gzipped engine WASM and 0.95 MB of gzipped game pack. These are comparison points for the duel. Start with a directional light, few material variants, baked surface detail and inexpensive background figures. Profile shader startup and the busiest shot before adding heavier shadows, particles or crowd animation. Reconsider rendering or delivery only if the exported duel cannot meet the agreed quality after a focused optimization pass.
